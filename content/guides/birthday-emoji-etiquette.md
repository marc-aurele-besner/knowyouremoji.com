Three birthday texts arrived at 9:14 AM on the same morning. The first one read "happy birthday 🎂🎈🎉". The second read "happy birthday 🎂 love you, hope the day is great". The third read "happy birthday 🎉🎉🎉". Same hour. Same recipient. Three completely different reads. The first one was a stack the sender had clearly been reusing for years; it landed as warm and slightly lazy. The second one was a sentence the sender actually wrote; it landed as the warmest text of the day. The third was pure confetti and no sentence; it landed as performative, like the sender was celebrating for the audience rather than for the recipient.

The birthday text is one of the highest-stakes small messages on the keyboard. It gets screenshotted. It gets forwarded to friend groups with a "look what [name] sent 💀" caption. It gets read at 7 AM with one eye open and re-read at 11 PM after drinks. The cost of sending a bad birthday text is small in the moment and enormous in the long run, because the recipient remembers which friend sent a real one and which friend sent the template.

## The shape of the birthday emoji stack

The birthday emoji vocabulary in 2026 is built out of five core glyphs and a handful of auxiliaries. Each one does a different job, and the stack you send announces how much time you spent on the text:

- **🎂 birthday cake.** The canonical birthday glyph. Reads as "I know this is your day, and I bothered to pick the birthday-specific emoji." Sends the clearest "birthday" signal on the keyboard. Soft, gentle, almost a sticker.
- **🎈 balloon.** The soft opener. A single 🎈 reads as warm and slightly wistful. Stacking 🎈🎈🎈 amplifies the soft register. Less birthday-specific than 🎂 but reads as "I am celebrating you gently."
- **🎉 party popper.** The loud, confetti-loaded default. Reads as "let's go" energy. Stacks well with anything. Lands as warm from a friend and obligatory from a colleague.
- **🥳 partying face.** The emotional version of 🎉. The face emoji carries warmth that 🎉 alone cannot. Reads as "I am excited for you" rather than "this is happening." Lands cleaner in 1:1 birthday texts and on milestone moments.
- **🎁 wrapped gift.** The gift-coded beat. Reads as "I wish I could give you something." Best for close friends and romantic partners. Risky in colleague or ex contexts because the gift-coded read lands as transactional or performative.

The auxiliaries layer in: ✨ sparkles for the "shine on your day" beat, 💖💗 the soft hearts for romantic warmth, 👏 or 🙌 for the celebration clap, 🥂 clinking glasses for the "drinks tonight" implicit invite, and 🎇🎆 the fireworks for the loud, milestone-aged moment.

The five core glyphs stack in any order. Most birthday texts in 2026 open with 🎂 because the cake anchors the meaning as birthday-specific; 🎉🥳🎈🎁 arrive as softeners and amplifiers. The stack the sender picks tells the recipient how much they wanted to do the birthday work.

## What 🎂🎈🎉 is doing in a birthday text

The 🎂🎈🎉 stack has been the default birthday text since around 2014. It is the lowest-effort warm birthday text on the keyboard, and it works in a tight band:

- **"happy birthday 🎂🎈🎉"** to a close friend → reads as warm. The cake anchors the birthday, the balloon adds softness, the popper adds the "let's go" beat. The recipient parses it as "they remembered, they sent the stack, they didn't write a sentence but the stack does the work." The friend forgives the missing sentence because the relationship has a backlog of real conversations.
- **"happy birthday 🎂🎈🎉"** to a coworker → reads as obligatory. The stack is the same one the coworker sends to twenty other people. The recipient parses it as "this is on the calendar, they sent the default, this is the corporate warmth." It does not age the sender because the coworker does not expect more, but it also does not register as warm.
- **"happy birthday 🎂🎈🎉"** to an ex → reads as confused. The stack carries no affection, no warmth, no closure. The ex parses it as "they didn't know what to send so they sent the default." The text ages both of them. Default to no text, or to a clear closure line.
- **"happy birthday 🎂🎈🎉"** to a family member → reads as efficient. The family member parses the stack as the sender doing the minimum on a busy morning. It does not warm the relationship, but it also does not hurt it. A sentence alongside the stack would do more.
- **"happy birthday 🎂🎈🎉"** to a situationship → reads as ambiguous. The stack does not register any romantic weight. The situationship parses it as "they remembered, they sent the friendly stack, they did not signal anything romantic." For a situationship that wants to stay friendly, the stack is fine. For one that wants to escalate, the stack reads as a missed opportunity.

The 🎂🎈🎉 stack is doing efficient warmth. It registers the moment without performing any specific affection. The right way to send it is paired with a sentence the recipient cannot find in any other text.

## What 🎂 alone does in a birthday text

A bare 🎂 is a different register than the stack. It reads as personal rather than templated:

- **"happy birthday 🎂"** to a close friend → reads as intimate. The single cake announces that the sender picked the birthday-specific emoji and stopped. The recipient parses it as the sender wanting to land the meaning without crowding the moment. The single 🎂 is the warm minimalist birthday text in 2026.
- **"happy birthday 🎂"** to a partner → reads as warm but maybe too brief. The single cake does the birthday work but does not register any romantic weight. Default to 🎂💖 or to a sentence with the cake. A partner who gets only 🎂 on their birthday reads it as the sender not bothering to perform the romance.
- **"happy birthday 🎂"** to a parent → reads as minimal. The single cake does the work, but parents parse the minimalism as "the kid was busy this morning." A sentence alongside the cake is what closes the loop. 🎂 + "love you, see you Sunday" does more than the cake alone.
- **"happy birthday 🎂"** to a colleague → reads as fine. The single cake is the polite minimum in a work context. It does not age the sender because the colleague does not expect more. It also does not warm the relationship.

The 🎂 alone is the birthday emoji that ages the least, because its job is so small. It registers that the sender noticed and picked the right emoji. The right way to send it is paired with whatever sentence the relationship warrants.

## What 🎉🥳 does in a birthday text

The 🎉🥳 pair is louder than 🎂🎈🎉. It registers energy rather than warmth:

- **"happy birthday 🎉🥳"** to a friend who loves loud celebration → reads as the right energy. The popper carries the "let's go" beat and the face emoji carries the emotional warmth. The recipient parses it as the sender matching the moment.
- **"happy birthday 🎉🥳"** to a friend who runs quiet → reads as too loud. The popper and the face over-perform the warmth for a recipient who prefers understated. Default to 🎂 or 🎈 or a sentence. Match the volume of the emoji to the volume of the recipient.
- **"happy birthday 🎉🥳"** to a colleague → reads as over-celebratory in a work context. The 🥳 reads as too familiar from a peer. Default to 🎂 or 🎉 alone. The 🥳 ages the sender in a work thread.
- **"happy birthday 🎉🥳"** to a crush → reads as a missed opportunity. The popper and the face do not register any romantic interest. The crush parses it as "they sent the friendly stack." For a crush who the sender wants to flirt with, default to 🎂💖 or to a sentence with one warm emoji.

The 🎉🥳 pair is doing celebratory energy rather than birthday-specific warmth. It works when the recipient's personality runs at that volume. It ages the sender when the recipient is quieter than the emoji stack.

## What 🎁 does in a birthday text

The 🎁 wrapped gift is the trickiest birthday emoji because it carries a gift-coded register that can land warmly or transactionally:

- **"happy birthday 🎁"** to a partner → reads as "I have something for you." The gift emoji carries the implicit promise of a present. The partner parses it as warm and as the sender hinting at a gift. Works in established relationships.
- **"happy birthday 🎁"** to a friend → reads as warm if the sender has actually bought something. The friend parses the gift emoji as "they got me something." Works in close friendships.
- **"happy birthday 🎁"** to a friend who is not receiving a gift → reads as performative. The friend parses the gift emoji as "they sent it without sending a gift." The 🎁 ages the sender because it implies a gift that did not arrive.
- **"happy birthday 🎁"** to a colleague → reads as transactional. The colleague parses the gift emoji as "they are implying a gift they did not send." Default to 🎂 or 🎉 alone. The 🎁 ages the sender in a work context.
- **"happy birthday 🎁"** to an ex → reads as confusing. The ex parses the gift emoji as "they are implying closure or affection they did not actually send." Default to no text. The 🎁 does not survive an ex-thread.

The 🎁 is the only birthday emoji that implies an action the sender may not be taking. Pair it with the actual gift, or skip it. The 🎁 without the gift ages the sender.

## Birthday messages by relationship

The same emoji stack reads completely differently depending on who is sending it. The rules that hold up across relationships:

- **Close friend.** 🎂🎈🎉 with a sentence that names a shared memory. The friend forgives the template stack because the sentence carries the actual warmth. The stack is fine on its own only if the friendship runs on emoji.
- **Best friend.** 🎂🥳💖 with a sentence. The best friend expects more than the default stack. Pair the cake and the face with a sentence that mentions a shared history. The best friend who gets only the default stack reads it as the sender not bothering.
- **Family member.** 🎂 with a sentence. Family parses the emoji stack as efficient but expects the sentence. A parent, sibling, or close relative wants to hear the sentence more than the stack. 🎂 + "love you, see you Sunday" does more than 🎂🎈🎉🎉 alone.
- **Romantic partner.** 🎂💖 with a sentence. A partner expects the romantic weight. The cake anchors the birthday, the heart carries the affection, and the sentence closes the loop. A partner who gets only 🎂 reads it as the sender not bothering to perform the romance.
- **Crush or situationship.** 🎂✨ or 🎂💖 with a sentence. The crush expects warmth without commitment. The cake does the birthday work, the ✨ or 💖 adds the soft interest, and the sentence names the moment. A crush who gets only 🎂🎈🎉 reads it as a missed opportunity.
- **Coworker or manager.** 🎂 with no other emoji, or 🎉 alone. A work context does not survive the multi-emoji stack. The single cake or the single popper does the polite minimum without over-performing.
- **Ex or former close friend.** No text, or a sentence that closes the door. An ex does not benefit from the birthday emoji stack. The stack lands as either confused or performative. Default to silence, or to a clear "happy birthday, hope it's a great year" without emoji.
- **Acquaintance or parasocial recipient.** 🎂 or 🎉. The acquaintance parses the polite emoji as fine. Do not stack more than one emoji in an acquaintance-thread. The stack ages the sender because the relationship has not built the warmth.

The relationship sets the ceiling on the emoji stack. Match the warmth of the stack to the warmth of the relationship. Sending too much ages the sender. Sending too little ages the recipient.

## The late birthday text dilemma

The late birthday text is its own etiquette problem. The recipient saw the absence of a text at midnight, and the sender is now sending something at 9 PM the next day, or worse, three days later:

- **Within 24 hours, before noon.** "happy birthday, sorry this is late 🎂" → reads as apologetic and warm. The recipient parses it as "they remembered once they woke up." The cake anchors the apology. The text closes the loop.
- **Within 24 hours, evening.** "happy birthday, hope it was a great one 🎂" → reads as slightly late but acceptable. The recipient has already had the day and is now hearing from the late sender. The text works because the sender acknowledged the lateness.
- **1-3 days late.** "happy birthday 🎂 sorry, this week was crazy" → reads as late. The recipient has moved on from the birthday and is now receiving a delayed text. The text needs a real reason for the lateness or it reads as the sender forgetting. Pair the cake with a sentence that explains the lateness and names the sender's care.
- **More than a week late.** "happy birthday 🎂 hope it was great" → reads as forgetting. The recipient parses the week-late text as the sender just now remembering. The text ages the sender unless it is paired with a real acknowledgment of the lateness and a plan to celebrate late.
- **Late birthday text with no acknowledgment of the lateness.** "happy birthday 🎂🎈🎉" sent four days late, no apology, no "sorry this is late" → reads as the sender not realizing how late they were. The recipient parses it as the sender not paying attention. Default to no text in this window, or to a clear "I know this is late" sentence alongside the emoji.

The late birthday text is harder than the on-time one. The sender is not just sending a birthday text. The sender is also acknowledging that they missed the day. Pair the cake with a sentence that names the lateness and the sender's care.

## Group chat birthday etiquette

The group chat birthday is its own genre. The sender has to balance warm individual attention with the public drop-in:

- **Drop 🎉🎉🎉 in the main group.** "happy birthday [name] 🎉🎉🎉" in the main group chat reads as warm but generic. The recipient parses it as "they remembered, they dropped the confetti." It works in established friend groups where the birthday drop-in is the norm.
- **Drop 🎂 in the main group.** "happy birthday [name] 🎂" in the main group reads as efficient. The cake anchors the birthday, and the recipient parses it as the sender doing the minimum. Pair with a sentence in the DM for warmth.
- **Drop a real sentence in the main group.** "happy birthday [name], hope the year is great, love you" → reads as the warm version. The sentence carries the warmth. The recipient parses it as the sender paying attention.
- **Skip the main group, send a DM.** When the relationship is close, the DM does more than the group drop-in. The recipient parses the DM as "they thought of me separately from the group." The DM with a sentence and a cake is warmer than the main group confetti.
- **Send both.** Drop a 🎉 in the main group and then a sentence with 🎂 in the DM. The recipient parses this as "they did the public thing and then they did the private thing." This is the warmest birthday move in 2026.

The group chat birthday text has to be calibrated to the group. In a friend group where the birthday drop-in is the norm, the confetti is fine. In a more reserved group, a sentence with the cake does more. Match the group energy.

## The Instagram and Facebook birthday post

The public birthday post is a different genre from the private text. The audience is wider, the permanence is longer, and the cost of a bad post is higher:

- **"happy birthday to the one who keeps me sane 🎂🎈🎉 love you"** on Instagram → reads as warm. The sentence anchors the post, the stack carries the warmth. The recipient parses it as the sender performing the birthday publicly, which most recipients enjoy. The post is safe because the sentence names the relationship.
- **"🎂🎈🎉🎉🎉"** as a caption-only post → reads as performative. The confetti without a sentence ages the sender. The audience parses it as the sender wanting credit for the birthday without writing the words.
- **"happy birthday 🎂"** as a one-line caption → reads as minimal. The audience parses it as the sender doing the minimum. A sentence would do more.
- **"another year older, another year wiser 🥳"** as a birthday post → reads as cliché. The cliché register ages the post. Default to a sentence that names the recipient rather than the cliché.
- **A photo dump with a 🎂 caption.** A photo dump of the recipient with a 🎂 or 🎉 caption reads as warm and personal. The photos do the work; the caption closes the loop.

The public birthday post has to do two jobs at once: name the recipient's day and announce the sender's care. A sentence that names the relationship does more than a stack of confetti. Match the warmth of the post to the publicness of the platform.

## Milestone birthdays (30, 40, 50, 60)

The milestone birthday has its own emoji rules. The stack the sender picks has to match the weight of the moment:

- **30th birthday.** A milestone without being heavy. The 🎉🥳✨ stack with a sentence about the new decade reads as warm. A 🎂 alone reads as too minimal. The 30th birthday wants a sentence that names the decade without being sad about it. "30 looks good on you 🥳" lands warmer than "welcome to your 30s 🎂".
- **40th birthday.** A heavier milestone. The 🎂🥂💫 stack with a sentence about the recipient's life works. A 🎉🥳🎉 stack alone reads as too loud for the gravity of the moment. The 40th birthday wants a sentence that acknowledges the milestone without joking about it. "40 and finally figured out 🥂" lands warmer than "over the hill 🎂".
- **50th birthday.** A serious milestone. The 🎂🥂✨ stack with a sentence that names the recipient's life works. A 🎉 alone reads as too thin. The 50th birthday wants a sentence that honors the recipient's decade. Default to a longer text than the default birthday text. A short paragraph does more than a stack of confetti.
- **60th birthday.** The milestone that wants the closest version of the sender. The 🎂🥂💖 stack with a paragraph that names the recipient's life and the relationship works. A stack alone reads as too thin. The 60th birthday wants the sender to write the real text, not the emoji stack.

The milestone birthday wants the sender to match the weight of the moment. A 21st birthday can be a 🎉🥳 stack with a "cheers to 21" sentence. A 50th birthday wants a paragraph. Match the weight of the emoji to the weight of the year.

## The "I almost forgot" birthday crossover

The "I almost forgot" birthday text is the apology-birthday crossover that trips up most people:

- **"happy birthday, sorry this is so late 🎂 I thought about you all day"** → reads as warm. The sender is acknowledging the lateness and the care in one breath. The cake does the birthday work; the sentence carries the warmth.
- **"happy birthday, I know I'm late 🎂🥺"** → reads as the apology crossover. The cake and the pleading face together read as "I am sorry I am late and I am celebrating you." Works in close friendships.
- **"happy birthday 🎂"** sent four days late with no acknowledgment → reads as the sender not realizing the lateness. The recipient parses it as forgetting. The text ages the sender.
- **"happy birthday, sorry, this week was insane 🎂"** sent two days late → reads as the sender being honest about the lateness. The recipient parses it as the sender caring and being busy. The text closes the loop.

The "I almost forgot" text has to acknowledge the lateness explicitly. The cake alone sent late reads as forgetting. Pair it with a sentence that names the lateness. The combo closes the loop the cake alone cannot.

## Where birthday emojis reliably fail

The birthday emoji fails in predictable ways. The contexts where the emoji ages the sender, and what to send instead:

1. **In a work group channel.** "happy birthday [name] 🎂🎈🎉" in a work Slack channel reads as the team doing the corporate warmth. The stack ages no one because the audience does not expect more. Pair the stack with a sentence if the birthday person is a close coworker. A single 🎂 in a work channel is the polite minimum.
2. **In a romantic-partner thread with no sentence.** "happy birthday 🎂🎈🎉" from a partner with no sentence reads as the partner not bothering. A partner expects the romantic weight. Pair the cake with 💖 and a sentence that names the relationship.
3. **In a family thread with the default stack.** A parent or sibling who gets only 🎂🎈🎉 reads it as the sender doing the minimum. Pair the stack with "love you" or with a longer sentence that names the relationship.
4. **In a brand's customer reply.** "happy birthday 🎂" from a brand in a customer-facing channel reads as the brand performing warmth the brand has not earned. Default to a sentence and a discount code. The 🎂 ages the brand the most.
5. **In an ex-thread with no closure.** "happy birthday 🎂" from an ex with no explanation reads as confused. The ex parses it as the sender wanting credit for the birthday without closing the door. Default to silence, or to a clear "happy birthday, hope it's a great year" without emoji.
6. **In a reply to a friend's vulnerable share.** A friend who shares "I am having a hard birthday" does not want 🎂🎈🎉. The friend wants acknowledgment. Default to "I hear you, want to talk?" and skip the emoji stack entirely.
7. **In a 30+ birthday thread without acknowledging the decade.** "happy 30th 🎂" with no sentence reads as the sender ignoring the milestone. Pair the cake with a sentence that names the decade. "30 looks good on you" lands warmer than 🎂 alone.
8. **Repeated many times in a row.** "🎂🎂🎂🎂🎈🎈🎈🎈🎉🎉🎉" as a stack of confetti reads as the sender over-performing the celebration. One cake and one popper do more than ten stacked emojis. Match the warmth of the stack to the warmth of the moment.
9. **At a recipient who doesn't use emojis.** A Boomer recipient who gets 🎂🎈🎉🎉🎉 from a Gen Z sender reads it as the sender speaking a language the Boomer does not parse. Default to "happy birthday" and a sentence, with no emoji, when the recipient does not use the register.
10. **In a late birthday text with no acknowledgment of the lateness.** The late birthday text without "sorry this is late" ages the sender. The recipient parses it as the sender forgetting. Pair the cake with a sentence that names the lateness.
11. **As a substitute for actually showing up.** "happy birthday 🎂" sent instead of attending the birthday dinner reads as the sender prioritizing the emoji over the relationship. The emoji is the punctuation; the showing up is the birthday. Do both, or skip the emoji.
12. **As sarcasm or irony at a real milestone.** "happy birthday 🎂 lol" reads as the sender mocking the recipient's age. The lol ages the birthday instantly. Drop the lol. The birthday is the birthday; the irony ages it.

## Replies that volley back

A few reliable options, ordered by how much energy the recipient wants to return:

- **Match with the same emoji.** "thanks 🎂🎈🎉" mirrors the sender's stack. Universal in friend threads and group chats where the stack was the warmth.
- **Soften with ❤️ or 💖.** When the sender sent a warm stack, mirror the love. ❤️ closes the loop in a way that maintains the warmth.
- **Escalate with the [🎂🎈 birthday-cake-balloon combo](/combo/birthday-cake-balloon).** When the recipient wants to return the warm birthday energy, the combo closes the loop. The combo reads as the soft "thank you for the warm birthday text" reply.
- **Escalate with the [🎂🎁 birthday-cake-gift combo](/combo/birthday-cake-gift).** When the sender mentioned a gift or hinted at one, the combo closes the loop. The combo reads as the gift-coded "I see the gift" reply.
- **Escalate with the [🎉✨ party-celebrate combo](/combo/party-celebrate).** When the sender sent the loud celebration stack, the combo mirrors the energy. The combo reads as the loud "thanks for the celebration" reply.
- **Escalate with the [🎂🥳 cake-party combo](/combo/cake-party).** When the sender paired the cake with the party face, the combo closes the loop. The combo reads as the warm "happy birthday to me" reply.
- **Send a sentence that names the sender's care.** "thank you, that means a lot" or "I love you, thanks for remembering" closes the thread without making the birthday bigger. The sentence does most of the work.
- **Defuse with "all good" or "thanks!"** When the recipient wants to clear the birthday text quickly, plain text is the cleanest reply. The thanks does the work.

A few replies to avoid: do not reply to a warm birthday text with a long essay (it over-extends the moment), do not reply to a default stack with another default stack (it doubles the laziness), and do not reply to a work birthday text with a romantic emoji (it ages both sender and recipient).

## Cross-platform reads

The same birthday stack reads differently depending on where the message lives:

- **On iMessage.** iMessage renders the emoji with Apple's full-color designs and the recipient sees the cake, the popper, and the face exactly as the sender intended. The 🎂🎈🎉 stack reads warm. The 🥳 reads warm. The 🎁 reads as the sender implying a gift. The recipient parses the emoji without distraction.
- **On WhatsApp.** WhatsApp renders its own emoji set, which is closer to Apple's than to Google's. The birthday stack reads warm. Family groups on WhatsApp often default to 🎂 + "feliz cumpleaños" rather than the full stack because the older recipients parse the cake more easily than they parse the popper.
- **On Slack.** Slack renders its own emoji set and Slack threads run on professional contexts where the birthday stack ages the sender. The single 🎂 is the work-safe default. The 🎁 rarely belongs in a Slack thread.
- **On Twitter/X.** Twitter renders its own emoji set and the birthday post lives in public. The 🎉🥳 stack reads ambient. The 🎂 reads as the soft opener. A sentence alongside the stack does more than the stack alone.
- **On Instagram DMs.** Instagram renders its own emoji set and DMs run across friend and partner channels. The 🎂💖 stack reads warm. The 🥳 reads warm. The 🎁 reads as the sender implying a gift.
- **On TikTok.** TikTok DMs run on the soft register the platform cultivates. The 🎂🥳 stack reads ambient. The 🎉🎉🎉 reads as over-performative on TikTok where the soft register dominates.
- **On Discord.** Discord renders its own emoji set and Discord threads run on friend and community channels. The 🎂🎈🎉 stack reads warm. The 🎁🎁🎁 reads as performative in a Discord thread where the gift emoji is rare.

The platform is part of the birthday. A 🎂🎈🎉 on iMessage and a 🎂 on Slack may land on the same friend, but the iMessage version reads intimate and the Slack version reads professional. Match the emoji to the platform the birthday text is being delivered on.

## Generational reads

The same birthday emoji lands on a Gen Z recipient, a millennial recipient, a Gen X recipient, and a Boomer recipient with very different reads:

- **Gen Z recipients (born ~1997-2012).** Gen Z parses every emoji in the birthday vocabulary without effort. The 🎂🎈🎉 is the universal warm default. The 🥳 is the soft warm upgrade. The 🎁 is the gift-coded beat. The 💖 is the romantic birthday heart. The ✨ is the "shine on your year" beat. Gen Z also invented the layered combo stacks — the [🎂✨ birthday-cake-sparkler combo](/combo/birthday-cake-sparkler) for the milestone birthday, the [🎂🕯️ birthday-cake-candle combo](/combo/birthday-cake-candle) for the candle-blowing moment. Gen Z is the most emoji-fluent generation on birthdays.
- **Millennial recipients (born ~1981-1996).** Millennials parse the 🎂, the 🎉, the 🥳, the 🎈, and the 🎁 easily. They tend to be slightly more cautious with the 🎁🎁🎁 triple-stack because the gift-coded register reads as performative to millennials. They are also the most likely to expect a sentence alongside the emoji when the relationship is close. The 🎂🎈🎉 alone in a millennial thread reads as fine for a casual friend and as too small for a close friend.
- **Gen X recipients (born ~1965-1980).** Gen X prefers 🎂 because the cake is universal. The 🎉 reads as fine. The 🥳 reads as slightly young to some Gen X recipients. The 🎁 reads as implying a gift that may not have arrived. When wishing a Gen X recipient a happy birthday, default to 🎂 and a sentence. Skip the 🥳 and the multi-emoji stack unless the recipient has shown they use the register.
- **Boomer recipients (born ~1946-1964).** Boomers parse 🎂 as the birthday emoji and parse the rest as confusing. A 🎂🎈🎉🎉🎉 stack to a Boomer reads as the sender speaking a language the Boomer does not parse. The safest Boomer birthday text is "happy birthday 🎂" with a sentence, in plain language, with the birthday named. The emoji part of the Boomer birthday should be 🎂 only, or no emoji at all.

The generational read matters because the birthday emoji has to land on the recipient the birthday is being sent to. A 🎂🎈🎉 aimed at a Boomer is the sender wishing in a language the Boomer does not parse. A single 🎂 aimed at a Gen Z recipient is fine but registers as the safe default rather than the warm one. Match the emoji to the recipient's generation, not to the sender's.

## FAQ
