You asked the group chat where the pizza order was going. Three people answered with a version of the same nothing, one of them sent 🤷, and everybody moved on. That is the emoji working exactly as designed. It is a whole sentence that costs nothing to send, and it says almost nothing.

The shrug also does four other jobs, and it does them with the same drawing. 🤷 is "not my problem" when it follows a colleague asking you to fix something. It is "my fault" when it follows "I dropped the keys". It is "fine, whatever you decide" when it follows a plan you do not like, and that is the reading that costs people the most, because it registers as agreement. It is "I refuse to care about this any more" in the specific voice of somebody posting about the state of the world.

Five sentences. One picture of a person with both arms in the air. The glyph carries none of the difference, so the words around it carry all of it, and the words are optional. That is why a shrug sent by itself lands differently every single time, and why the same sender can send it twice in one afternoon and be understood two opposite ways.

The full reference entry for the glyph is at [/emoji/person-shrugging](/emoji/person-shrugging). What follows is the part that page cannot hold: which sentence is yours, what the shrug does in each of the channels people actually use it in, and which of its neighbours on the keyboard is the one you meant.

## The shrug was a text joke before it was an emoji

For most of the 2000s the shrug was typed, not tapped. `¯\_(ツ)_/¯` was a small figure drawn in punctuation: the (ツ) is the head, the ¯ and \_/ are the arms, and the whole thing is a person holding their hands up. It spread across forums and timelines faster than any of the symbols around it, partly because it was funny and partly because it fit in a text message without needing an image.

Unicode 9.0 added the actual drawing in June 2016, in the release that also brought the rolling-laughing face, the thinking face and the eye roll. U+1F937 is called PERSON SHRUGGING, and the facepalm arrived in the same set. The translation from punctuation to picture was not neutral. The typed shrug always came with a sentence attached, usually a joke about the sender's own helplessness, and typing it out meant the sender had already committed to the words. The emoji has no sentence and no sender to blame, which is exactly why it spread so fast and exactly why it is ambiguous now.

Timing helped. 2016 is when texting stopped being SMS and became iMessage, WhatsApp, Messenger and Slack all at once, and a glyph meaning "no answer available, please stop asking" turned out to be the first emoji with a real job in a work chat. It became the punctuation mark for the end of a question. Teams still use it that way.

Two later additions changed the shrug itself. The man and woman shrugging variants arrived in Emoji 11.0 in 2018, built on the generic person-standing character rather than on the shrug, which is part of why the drawing still looks a little different depending on whose phone it is. The five skin tone versions came through in the 2020 wave of Unicode tone modifiers. Around the shrug, the keyboard also offers a set of neighbours that answer the same question with a different temperature: the [🤨 raised eyebrow](/emoji/face-with-raised-eyebrow), the [🤦 facepalm](/emoji/person-facepalming), the [🙄 eye roll](/emoji/face-with-rolling-eyes), and the [💁 tipping hand](/emoji/person-tipping-hand).

The gendered variants never landed the same way. 🤷‍♀️ was more often read as softer and more willing to take the blame, 🤷‍♂️ as blunter and finished with the question. That split is a stereotype rather than a fact about the drawings, but it is a difference people actually felt, which is why choosing a gendered variant in a thread where feelings are involved was a small decision with a long tail.

## Five sentences, one drawing

### 🤷 "I have no idea."

The literal reading, and the default one. The shrug as a claim about the state of the sender's knowledge, with no blame attached to anybody.

- **"where do we eat tonight 🤷"** in a chat where three people know and one does not.
- **"what time does it start 🤷"** from somebody who has not opened the invite.
- **"did it get fixed 🤷"** as a genuine status check.

This is the one case where the picture is doing exactly what it looks like, and it is why a bare shrug is usually harmless. It closes a question without inventing an answer.

### 🤷 "Not my problem."

The deflection, and the oldest thing about the gesture. The arms go up because the sender is physically handing the thing back to the room.

- **"that's between you and the client 🤷"**
- **"not my department 🤷"**
- **"can you ask the PM 🤷"**

This reading only exists because of what came before it. A shrug after a question is an answer. A shrug after an assignment is a refusal. Same picture, and the sentence before it is the whole difference.

### 🤷 "My fault."

The cheap apology, and the reading that catches people out, because the shrug gesture looks a little like a small bow.

- **"i dropped them at the bar 🤷"**
- **"yeah that was me 🤷"**
- **"forget it 🤷"**

A shrug here does not apologise. It admits and then stops. The apology emoji is [🙏 folded hands](/emoji/folded-hands), and a real apology is a sentence with a verb in it. "i dropped them at the bar 🤷" lands on the person who now has to find the keys as an admission that nobody is going to.

### 🤷 "Fine, whatever you decide."

The hollow agreement, and the most expensive of the five.

- **"sure 🤷"** under a plan she does not like.
- **"ok 🤷"** under a Saturday that does not work.
- **"yep 🤷"** under a shift nobody volunteered for.

A shrug after "sure" is technically valid consent and emotionally empty. The person reading it cannot tell whether the sender agreed or simply stopped arguing, and they are right not to be able to tell, because the sender did not say. This is where a shrug breaks a plan, a shift, and sometimes a relationship. The harm is not in the emoji. It is in the agreement nobody actually gave.

### 🤷 "I refuse to care."

The world-ending shrug, and the one with the most confident posture.

- **"we're all cooked 🤷"**
- **"anyway 🤷"** dropped at the end of a rant.
- **"the economy is fine 🤷"**

Here the shrug is being used as a period. It says the sender has stopped, not that the sender has no idea, and it is usually funny on purpose. The risk is that it is funny to the sender and read as indifference by somebody who was being serious two messages earlier.

## The same shrug, six ways

| The message                            | What it reads as                  |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| "where do we eat tonight 🤷"           | I have no idea                    |
| "that's between you and the client 🤷" | Not my problem                    |
| "i dropped the keys 🤷"                | My fault, and nobody is fixing it |
| "sure 🤷"                              | Fine, whatever you decide         |
| "we're all cooked 🤷"                  | I refuse to care                  |
| "no clue, ask her 🤷"                  | I know, and I am not telling you  |

Six messages, one glyph, and every line is decided by the four words before the emoji. That is the whole technique and the whole hazard.

## The shrug is not the raised eyebrow, and it is not the facepalm

The [🤨 face with raised eyebrow](/emoji/face-with-raised-eyebrow) is one tap away in every picker and it does something different. The raised eyebrow has an object. It questions one specific claim and waits for an answer: "you said eight 🤷" is not a question, "you said eight 🤨" is. The shrug questions everything at once and then moves on, which is why a shrug closes a conversation and a raised eyebrow keeps one open.

The [🤦 facepalm](/emoji/person-facepalming) answers a different question entirely. It says "I cannot", and it nearly always points at the sender's own situation or at something the sender just watched happen. "i told you it would work 🤦" is aimed at the sender. A shrug is aimed at nobody. When the shrug is a straight line between you and the other person, the facepalm is the version that keeps the problem in the room.

The [🙄 eye roll](/emoji/face-with-rolling-eyes) is the other "I do not care", and the difference is aim. An eye roll points at somebody's behavior, at the thing that just happened. A shrug points at the decision, at the group, at the question. In a group chat the 🙄 lands on the person who spoke; the 🤷 lands on everyone. The [dismissive emoji hierarchy](/guides/the-dismissive-emoji-hierarchy) has the full map of the faces that sit on that end of the keyboard.

Then there are the quieter faces. The [😐 neutral face](/emoji/neutral-face) and the [😑 expressionless face](/emoji/expressionless) are silence, and silence is not a handoff. A shrug actively moves something to somebody else. The [🫠 melting face](/emoji/melting-face) is the emoji for language giving up, which is a different failure from a shrug and gets its own argument in [the melting face guide](/guides/what-does-melting-face-mean). The [❓ question mark](/emoji/question-mark) is the one that actually asks, and the [🤔 thinking face](/emoji/thinking-face) is the one that says give me a second. And the [💁 person tipping hand](/emoji/person-tipping-hand) is the shrug with the volume up, offering a service or waving a problem off with a smirk.

## Where the shrug reliably fails

1. **As a sign-off on work you own.** "done 🤷" under a ticket you just closed. The reader gets a shrug where they expected ownership, and the shrug reads as "I am not accountable for this." The same message with a subject line and a next step lands as a professional.
2. **As a hollow agreement.** "sure 🤷" under a plan you did not agree to. It is the failure that makes people stop asking, because nobody wants to be the one who said yes to a shrug.
3. **As an apology.** "sorry 🤷" is not an apology. It is a confession with no repair attached, and the other person is left holding the problem.
4. **Under bad news.** Somebody writes they lost their job and you send "oh no 🤷". Even "that sucks 🤷" lands as "and I do not care which."
5. **When the question was aimed at you.** "where were you? 🤷" is an evasion. The question had an addressee and the shrug declines to be an answer to it.
6. **In a thread with a manager.** "not sure 🤷" reads as a refusal to think about it. A manager reading it learns that you have no view, which is information they will use later.
7. **On a date.** "idk 🤷" in reply to "so where do we stand" reads as avoidant. The thread gets thinner, and the shrug is the reason people stop asking.
8. **In a family thread.** "ask your dad 🤷" reads as an abdication of the entire conversation, and the family chat goes quiet for a day.
9. **As a reply to good news.** Somebody says they got the job and you send 🤷. Even sent in the "we're all cooked" register, it is a strange thing to hand somebody's good news.
10. **When the answer matters.** If the thread is waiting on a number, a time, a decision, a yes or a no, the shrug never delivers one. It is the one reaction that cannot be used as a substitute for the thing being asked for.
11. **To somebody who asked you a direct question.** The shrug does not ask. If you need information, [❓](/emoji/question-mark) asks and 🤷 does not, and "idk" plus a question mark beats a shrug plus silence every time.
12. **Anything serious.** A dispute, a complaint, a safety issue, a refund, a bereavement. The shrug's entire power is that it is unserious enough to send in half a second. It cannot carry a serious message.

## Replies that volley back

- **Shrug it back.** Replying to a 🤷 with a 🤷 is the correct answer when the first one was a reaction and you have nothing to add. Matching the energy is the point.
- **Ask for the thing you actually need.** When the sender shrugs something you need, use [❓](/emoji/question-mark) and name it: "what time? ❓". A question beats a shrug because it moves the thread forward.
- **Escalate the bewilderment.** [🤷❓ the shrug-question-mark combo](/combo/shrug-question-mark) is the sender-side version, for when you genuinely have no idea and are not sure why you are being asked. It reads as confusion rather than dismissal.
- **Return the wave-off.** [🤷💁 the shrug-whatever combo](/combo/shrug-tipping-hand) is the dismissive pair, for the thread that has already decided the problem is somebody else's.
- **Match the resignation.** [🤷🙃 the shrug-upside-down combo](/combo/shrug-upside-down) is the chaotic acceptance beat, "it is what it is." It works between friends and it does not work with anybody who came to you for a decision.
- **Go soft and floating.** [🤷☁️ the shrug-cloud combo](/combo/person-shrugging-cloud-v2) is for the day where you are genuinely adrift, a different mood entirely from the shrug's usual flatness.
- **Say the impossible out loud.** When a shrug landed as "I refuse," 🤦 is the honest alternative. It is directed at the situation, it carries embarrassment instead of indifference, and it keeps the joke between you.
- **Push back on one thing.** When the shrug was aimed at a specific claim you doubt, 🤨 questions that claim and leaves everything else alone.
- **Confirm with a word, not a shrug.** When somebody shrugs and you need them to actually agree, "yes, let's do that" is worth more than any glyph on the keyboard.
- **Defuse with a sentence.** If the reply itself might be misread, follow the picture with words. "shrugging but yes, I'm in 🤷" cannot land as refusal, because the sentence is doing the work the arms cannot.

## Who reads the shrug, and where

Gen Z sends the shrug as a default reaction and as a punchline about things that cannot be fixed. "we're all cooked 🤷" is a whole genre of post on its own, and in that register the emoji is closer to a laugh than to a shrug. Millennials tend to send it deliberately, as "your call" or "I have no opinion and I am not going to develop one." Older senders read it more literally, as the physical gesture, and use it for "I do not know, ask somebody else," which is often the honest meaning.

Platforms change it more than generations do. On Slack and Discord the shrug lives in the reaction bar, one tap, no words, which is the situation where the ambiguity is worst because there is nothing at all to disambiguate it. A shrug reaction to a plan can be read as agreement by whoever is running the meeting, and that is a real problem with the emoji in a work channel. On iMessage and WhatsApp the shrug is a full message in a picker, and people almost always put at least one word in front of it, which is why it mostly works there. On X and Twitter it is the reply-grave emoji, the entire answer to a take nobody has time to argue with.

The pattern worth remembering: the more words that travel with the shrug, the less damage it can do. The shrug was invented as a shorthand for a sentence nobody wanted to write, and it will keep working as long as people remember it is shorthand for five different sentences rather than one.

## FAQ

**What does 🤷 mean in a text?**
It most often means "I have no idea," but the same glyph means "not my problem," "my fault," "fine, whatever you decide," and "I refuse to care" depending on the sentence in front of it. The emoji itself does not choose. The words do.

**Is 🤷 rude?**
On its own, rarely. It is the sentence around it that decides, and the reading that causes real damage is the hollow agreement, where "sure 🤷" registers as a yes you never gave. A shrug under bad news, or under a question that was aimed at you, is the other one worth avoiding.

**What is the difference between 🤷 and 🤨?**
The raised eyebrow questions one specific claim and waits for an answer. The shrug questions everything at once and moves on. In practice the 🤨 keeps a conversation open and the 🤷 closes it.

**What does 🤷 mean at work?**
Usually "I have no idea" or "nobody knows," and it is a normal way to close a question nobody can answer. It fails when it stands in for ownership ("done 🤷"), for agreement ("sure 🤷"), or for a decision you were asked to make. Use a sentence in all three.

**What does 🤷 mean from a guy or a girl?**
Nothing gender-specific by default. The neutral 🤷 carries no gender at all. The gendered variants do, mostly by stereotype: 🤷‍♀️ reads softer and 🤷‍♂️ reads blunter to people who notice the difference, which is usually not worth it in a thread where feelings are involved.

**How do you reply to a 🤷?**
Shrug it back if it was a reaction. Ask a question if you need an answer. Match the resignation with [🤷🙃](/combo/shrug-upside-down) if the thread has already given up on something. And when the reply matters more than the vibe, write the sentence, because a shrug is never going to be quoted back to you as an agreement.

Before you send one, name the sentence out loud. Sometimes it is "I have no idea" and the shrug is perfect. Sometimes it is "I do not care" and the shrug is a confession. And sometimes it is "sure, whatever you decide," and the shrug is the cheapest way ever invented to say yes to something you do not want to do. The drawing is the same in all three, and only the words tell them apart.
